## 1. Thesis and narrative arc

The chapter is organized as a **mechanistic → data-driven spectrum**. It opens by asking what a mathematical model in biotechnology is *for* (prediction, design, control, mechanistic understanding) and what distinguishes models whose parameters carry physical meaning from models that learn structure from data. It then walks the reader from first-principles models toward learned models, and closes by arguing that the productive frontier is **hybrid** (mechanism-informed machine learning).

Two biological systems recur so the reader sees the same biology through different mathematical lenses:

- **A CHO fed-batch monoclonal-antibody (mAb) bioreactor** — introduced as a mechanistic ODE in the kinetics section (§2), then revisited as a data-driven and hybrid forecasting problem in the deep-learning section (§5) and the hybrid section (§6).
- **E. coli cell-free transcription–translation (TX-TL)** — the substrate for the gene-expression "ladder" (§4).

Flux balance analysis (§3, urea cycle) stands on its own as the constraint-based interlude between dynamic kinetic models and gene-expression models.

## 3. Section outline

Each pillar is anchored by one original Julia worked example whose figures are regenerated from code in `code/`.

### §1 Introduction
- What a model is for in biotechnology; the mechanistic ↔ data-driven spectrum.
- Cross-cutting themes introduced once and reused: model identifiability, parameter estimation, validation vs. calibration, extrapolation.
- Roadmap and the two recurring systems.
- *No worked example.*

### §2 Kinetic / mechanistic ODE models
- Enzyme kinetics (Michaelis–Menten derivation from the quasi-steady-state assumption), Monod growth, mass-action networks, Luedeking–Piret product formation.
- Build up to the **CHO fed-batch mAb model**: 7 states (volume, viable biomass, glucose, glutamine, lactate, ammonia, mAb), Monod kinetics with by-product inhibition, glucose-triggered feed policy.
- **Worked example:** simulate the CHO fed-batch ODE; produce trajectory figures. This model also generates the synthetic training data used in §5.
- Sidebar: the Hockin–Mann coagulation cascade as an example of large mechanistic reaction networks.
- Derivations inline: MM quasi-steady-state; Monod; mass balances for fed-batch with changing volume.
- Anchor refs: Michaelis & Menten 1913; Monod 1949; Luedeking & Piret 1959; Hockin–Mann.

### §3 Flux balance analysis
- Metabolism as a chemical factory; the stoichiometric matrix; steady-state mass balance `S·v = 0`; the FBA linear program; exchange/transport reactions; flux variability analysis; brief note on genome-scale models and SVD structure of S.
- **Worked example:** **urea cycle FBA** — construct S, set bounds, solve the LP, interpret the optimal flux distribution. Adapted from the 5430 repo (`src/` modules + urea-cycle notebook).
- Derivation inline: `S·v = 0` from open-species mole balances under steady-state, constant-volume, no-accumulation assumptions (the Palsson constraints).
- Anchor refs: Orth, Thiele & Palsson 2010 ("What is FBA?"); Thiele & Palsson 2013; Heirendt 2019 (COBRA).

### §4 Gene-expression models — the ladder (centerpiece)
Four rungs of increasing biophysical granularity, all on E. coli cell-free expression, with **cell-free vs whole-cell** threaded through:

1. **Hill / phenomenological control functions** — saturating activation/repression input functions; cooperativity. *Implemented directly (trivial).*
2. **Effective biophysical model** (Adhikari, Vilkhovoy, Vadhin, Lim, Varner 2020, *Front. Bioeng. Biotechnol.* 8:539081) — pseudo-enzyme (Michaelis–Menten-like) kinetic limits × partition-function control functions; coarse-grained, tractable parameter count; cell-free deGFP. *Compact reimplementation, adaptable from the gluconate framework.*
3. **Structured-promoter model** (gluconate biosensor paper, `Gluconate-Sensor-Model-Paper`) — McClure 4-step TX/TL mechanism + Boltzmann partition function over promoter states + load-dependent resource depletion; 11-state ODE; gluconate dose-response as validation. *Adapted from the local repo.*
4. **Cybernetic / resource-allocation "choice"** (Kompala model, `varnerlab/Kompala-Model-LP-Paper`) — gene expression as an optimal allocation decision; mixed-substrate diauxic growth; the bridge to whole-cell. *Adapted from the Kompala LP repo.*
- **Worked example:** the four-rung ladder applied to cell-free / mixed-substrate systems, showing how prediction and interpretability change as granularity increases.
- Derivations inline: partition-function / statistical-mechanics control (Ackers 1982); McClure 4-step mechanism → kinetic limits; resource depletion; cybernetic objective.
- Anchor refs: Ackers et al. 1982; McClure 1980; Moon et al. 2012; Adhikari et al. 2020; Kompala et al. 1986.

### §5 Deep time-series models
- Why data-driven sequence models; sequential data and the limits of mechanistic models when mechanism is unknown or too costly.
- **RNN → LSTM → S4/SSM** spine: Elman RNN and backpropagation-through-time; vanishing/exploding gradients; LSTM gating; structured state-space models (HiPPO-LegS, bilinear discretization) for long sequences.
- **Worked example (original):** **S4 vs LSTM head-to-head** forecasting multivariate CHO mAb bioreactor trajectories. Training data are synthetic, generated by the §2 mechanistic model. Implemented with Flux/Lux (LSTM via the framework; S4 as a custom HiPPO-LegS layer trained in the same ecosystem). Compare multi-step forecast accuracy and behavior vs. horizon.
- **Brief subsection:** attention/Transformers — where they fit for sequence modeling and when they would be preferred; conceptual only, no separate worked example.
- Derivations inline: BPTT; LSTM gate equations; S4 continuous→discrete (bilinear) state-space recurrence and the HiPPO construction.
- Anchor refs: Hochreiter & Schmidhuber 1997; Gu, Goel & Ré 2022 (S4); Vaswani et al. 2017 (attention, brief).

### §6 Hybrid models and outlook
- Mechanistic+ML / gray-box / scientific machine learning; using mechanism to constrain or augment learned models.
- **Worked example:** **CHO mechanistic+ML hybrid** — contrast pure-mechanistic, pure-data (from §5), and a hybrid forecast on the CHO system; show the data-efficiency / extrapolation trade-off.
- Outlook: identifiability and data scarcity in biotech, foundation/sequence models, digital twins for bioprocesses.

## 4. Repository and code organization

Mirrors the conventions of `SA-Binding-Generation-Study` and `Gluconate-Sensor-Model-Paper` (single Julia entry point, `src/` modules, sections-based LaTeX), adapted to the author's requested `chapter/` + `chapter/sections/` layout.

```
MRW-BTC4-Chapter-Varner/
├── chapter/
│   ├── Chapter.tex                 # main; article class; \input{sections/...}
│   ├── sections/
│   │   ├── introduction.tex
│   │   ├── kinetics.tex
│   │   ├── fba.tex
│   │   ├── geneexpression.tex
│   │   ├── deeplearning.tex
│   │   ├── hybrid.tex
│   │   └── appendix.tex            # derivations that are too long for the main text
│   ├── figures/                    # final PDFs referenced by \includegraphics
│   ├── References.bib
│   └── Makefile                    # pdflatex → bibtex → pdflatex ×2; clean; (optional) figures
├── code/
│   ├── Include.jl                  # single entry point: activates env, loads src/
│   ├── Project.toml                # committed
│   ├── Manifest.toml               # committed for reproducibility (override default .gitignore)
│   ├── src/                        # shared utilities (IO, plotting theme, helpers)
│   ├── kinetics/                   # CHO mechanistic ODE + figures
│   ├── fba/                        # urea cycle FBA (adapted from 5430 repo)
│   ├── geneexpression/             # the four-rung ladder
│   ├── deeplearning/               # LSTM + S4 (Flux/Lux) + hybrid on CHO data
│   ├── data/                       # CHO synthetic trajectories, cell-free data, urea-cycle network
│   └── figs/                       # generated figures → copied/symlinked into chapter/figures
├── docs/superpowers/{specs,plans}/
└── README.md, LICENSE
```

- Each worked example is a runnable script that regenerates its figures. Running `code/Include.jl` then the per-pillar scripts reproduces every figure in the chapter.
- Source code from the four origin repos is **vendored and adapted** into the matching `code/` subfolder (all author's own MIT-licensed work), then wired to the shared `Include.jl` / `Project.toml`.
- `Manifest.toml` will be committed (the repo is an application, not a package); the current `.gitignore` rule for `Manifest*.toml` will be relaxed for `code/Manifest.toml`.

## 5. Build system

A `Makefile` in `chapter/` modeled on the Gluconate paper's Makefile:
- `make` / `make pdf` → `pdflatex Chapter` → `bibtex Chapter` → `pdflatex` ×2.
- `make clean` → remove LaTeX aux/output artifacts.
- `make figures` (optional) → invoke the Julia figure scripts to regenerate `chapter/figures/`.

## 8. Risks, scope guards, and possible cuts

- **Scope is ambitious** (6 sections, 4 worked examples, derivations inline) for ~4.5 weeks. It is feasible because most code already exists in the source repos and is being adapted, not written from scratch.
- **Highest-effort items:** the §4 gene-expression ladder (four rungs) and the §5 S4 implementation in Flux/Lux. The S4/HiPPO-LegS layer has no turnkey framework implementation and must be written as a custom layer.
- **De-risking cuts, in priority order if time runs short:** (a) make the cybernetic/Kompala rung review-only (cite, do not re-run); (b) lighten the §6 hybrid example to a conceptual figure; (c) reduce ladder rung 2 (Adhikari) to a reused figure rather than a fresh run. None of these affect the chapter's spine.

## 9. Out of scope

- Producing the exact publisher-formatted manuscript (we use plain `article`; reformat on acceptance/template receipt).
- A Transformer/attention worked example (conceptual mention only).
- Real experimental CHO data (synthetic from the mechanistic model only).
- Genome-scale FBA beyond the urea-cycle example (mentioned, not computed).
