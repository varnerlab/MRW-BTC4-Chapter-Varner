\subsection{Quasi-Steady-State Derivation of the Michaelis--Menten Rate Law}
\label{sec:mm_derivation}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Consider the single-enzyme mechanism
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_mechanism}
  E + S \;\underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}}\; ES
  \;\overset{k_2}{\longrightarrow}\; E + P,
\end{equation}
where $E$, $S$, $ES$, and $P$ denote free enzyme, substrate, enzyme--substrate
complex, and product, respectively.  Writing mass-action balances for each
species:
\begin{align}
  \frac{d[E]}{dt}  &= -k_1[E][S] + (k_{-1} + k_2)[ES], \label{eq:app_dE}\\
  \frac{d[ES]}{dt} &=  k_1[E][S] - (k_{-1} + k_2)[ES], \label{eq:app_dES}\\
  \frac{d[S]}{dt}  &= -k_1[E][S] + k_{-1}[ES],          \label{eq:app_dS}\\
  \frac{d[P]}{dt}  &=  k_2[ES].                          \label{eq:app_dP}
\end{align}
The total enzyme concentration $[E]_T = [E] + [ES]$ is conserved, so
$[E] = [E]_T - [ES]$.

\paragraph{The quasi-steady-state assumption (QSSA).}
When the enzyme is present at a concentration much lower than the substrate
($[E]_T \ll [S]_0$), the complex $ES$ reaches a pseudo-steady state on a
fast time scale relative to the depletion of $S$.  Setting
$d[ES]/dt \approx 0$ in Eq.~\eqref{eq:app_dES} gives:
\begin{equation}
  k_1([E]_T - [ES])[S] = (k_{-1} + k_2)[ES].
\end{equation}
Solving for $[ES]$:
\begin{equation}
  [ES]\bigl(k_1[S] + k_{-1} + k_2\bigr) = k_1[E]_T[S],
\end{equation}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_ES}
  [ES] = \frac{k_1[E]_T[S]}{k_1[S] + k_{-1} + k_2}
        = \frac{[E]_T[S]}{[S] + \dfrac{k_{-1}+k_2}{k_1}}.
\end{equation}
Defining the Michaelis constant
\begin{equation}\label{eq:KM_def}
  K_M \equiv \frac{k_{-1} + k_2}{k_1},
\end{equation}
Eq.~\eqref{eq:app_ES} becomes $[ES] = [E]_T[S]/(K_M + [S])$.

\paragraph{The rate law.}
The product formation rate (velocity) $v = d[P]/dt = k_2[ES]$ is therefore:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_MM}
  v = \frac{k_2[E]_T[S]}{K_M + [S]} = \frac{V_{\max}[S]}{K_M + [S]},
\end{equation}
where $V_{\max} \equiv k_2[E]_T$.  This is the Michaelis--Menten equation
\cite{MichaelisMenten1913}.

\paragraph{Limiting behavior.}
\begin{itemize}
  \item $[S] \ll K_M$: $v \approx (V_{\max}/K_M)[S]$, a first-order dependence
    on substrate with apparent first-order rate constant $k_{\rm cat}/K_M =
    k_2/K_M$.
  \item $[S] \gg K_M$: $v \approx V_{\max}$, zero-order in substrate; all
    enzyme active sites are saturated.
  \item $[S] = K_M$: $v = V_{\max}/2$, the defining operational meaning of
    $K_M$.
\end{itemize}

\paragraph{Validity of the QSSA.}
The QSSA is formally valid when $[E]_T \ll K_M + [S]_0$, where $[S]_0$ is
the initial substrate concentration (note this is a tighter condition than the
classical $[E]_T \ll [S]_0$, which neglects the contribution of $K_M$).
Under these conditions the time scale for
$ES$ to equilibrate ($\tau_{ES} \sim 1/(k_1[S]+k_{-1}+k_2)$) is much shorter
than the substrate depletion time ($\tau_S \sim [S]_0/V_{\max}$), justifying
the pseudo-steady-state approximation.

\subsection{Derivation of the Steady-State Flux-Balance Constraint
            \texorpdfstring{$\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{v}=\mathbf{0}$}{S v = 0}}
\label{sec:fba_derivation}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Consider a well-mixed intracellular compartment of volume $V$ containing
$m$ metabolite species and $r$ enzymatic and transport reactions.
Let $x_i$ (mol) denote the moles of species $i$ inside the compartment.
An open-species mole balance for species $i$ accounts for chemical
conversion, transport across the compartment boundary, and dilution by
cell growth:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_fba_mole_balance}
  \frac{dx_i}{dt}
    = \sum_{j=1}^{r} S_{ij}\,v_j\,V
      - \mu\,x_i,
      \qquad i = 1, \ldots, m,
\end{equation}
where $S_{ij}$ is the stoichiometric coefficient of species $i$ in reaction $j$
(negative for reactants, positive for products), $v_j$ is the volumetric
reaction rate of reaction $j$ (mol per unit volume per unit time), and $\mu$
is the specific growth rate.  The term $S_{ij}\,v_j\,V$ is the molar rate of
production of species $i$ by reaction $j$; the term $\mu\,x_i$ accounts for the
dilution of intracellular species as the cell grows and the compartment volume
increases.

Expressing concentrations $c_i = x_i / V$ and assuming constant volume
(constant cell size during balanced growth), Eq.~\eqref{eq:app_fba_mole_balance}
becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_fba_conc}
  \frac{dc_i}{dt}
    = \sum_{j=1}^{r} S_{ij}\,v_j - \mu\,c_i,
    \qquad i = 1, \ldots, m,
\end{equation}
or in matrix form
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_fba_matrix}
  \frac{d\mathbf{c}}{dt} = \mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{v} - \mu\,\mathbf{c}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Separation of time scales.}
Metabolic fluxes equilibrate on time scales of seconds to minutes; the
cell-growth time scale $\tau_\mu = \mu^{-1}$ is typically hours.  Consequently,
$|\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{v}| \gg |\mu\,\mathbf{c}|$ for most intracellular
metabolites, and the dilution term is negligible relative to the metabolic
production and consumption terms.  On the fast metabolic time scale
$\mathbf{c}$ therefore relaxes to a pseudo-steady state satisfying
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_fba_pss}
  \frac{d\mathbf{c}}{dt} \approx \mathbf{0}.
\end{equation}

\paragraph{The steady-state constraint.}
Substituting Eq.~\eqref{eq:app_fba_pss} into Eq.~\eqref{eq:app_fba_matrix}
and dropping the negligible dilution term gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:app_Sv0}
  \boxed{\mathbf{S}\,\mathbf{v} = \mathbf{0}.}
\end{equation}
This is the fundamental steady-state mass-balance constraint of flux balance
analysis: the stoichiometric matrix $\mathbf{S}$ maps the flux vector
$\mathbf{v}$ to zero, so $\mathbf{v}$ must lie in the (right) null space
$\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{S})$.

For a network with $r > m$ (more reactions than metabolites) the null space
has positive dimension $r - \mathrm{rank}(\mathbf{S}) > 0$, meaning that
infinitely many flux vectors satisfy Eq.~\eqref{eq:app_Sv0}.  Flux balance
analysis resolves this underdeterminacy by selecting the element of
$\mathcal{N}(\mathbf{S})$ that maximizes a biologically motivated linear
objective, subject to inequality bounds on individual fluxes, as described
in Section~\ref{sec:fba}.

\subsection{Derivation of the Hill Input Function}
\label{sec:hill_derivation}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

Consider a promoter with $n$ identical, independent binding sites for a
transcription factor (TF) monomer $X$.  In the cooperative limit each of the
$n$ sites must be occupied simultaneously for the promoter to be active (all-or-nothing
binding).  Let $p$ denote the probability that any single site is occupied.
At equilibrium $p = x / (K_d + x)$, where $x$ is the free TF concentration
and $K_d$ is the microscopic dissociation constant.  The probability that
all $n$ sites are simultaneously occupied is
\begin{equation}
  P_{\rm on} = p^n = \frac{x^n}{(K_d + x)^n}.
\end{equation}

In the all-or-nothing (two-state) approximation, intermediate partially-bound
states are neglected and the bound fraction takes the Hill form
$P_{\mathrm{on}} \approx x^n/(K^n + x^n)$, with the half-saturation constant
$K \approx K_d$.  This is the activating Hill function with $n$ as the cooperativity
(Hill) coefficient and $K$ as the half-maximal activation concentration
\cite{Alon2006}.  The same derivation via a thermodynamic (Boltzmann)
partition function over all $2^n$ binding states yields
\begin{equation}
  f_{\rm act}(x) = \frac{(x/K)^n}{1 + (x/K)^n} = \frac{x^n}{K^n + x^n},
\end{equation}
confirming the form in Eq.~\eqref{eq:hill_act}.  The repressing Hill function
$f_{\rm rep}(x) = 1 - f_{\rm act}(x) = K^n/(K^n + x^n)$ describes the
complementary case in which TF binding occludes the promoter.  When $n = 1$
the functions reduce to simple hyperbolic (Michaelis-type) saturation; for
$n > 1$ the response becomes progressively more switch-like, approaching a
step function at $x = K$ as $n \to \infty$.

\subsection{C1-FFL Sign-Sensitive Delay and I1-FFL Pulse Analysis}
\label{sec:c1ffl_analysis}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

\paragraph{C1-FFL sign-sensitive delay.}
In the coherent type-1 FFL with AND gate at $Z$, the output is governed by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:c1ffl_Y}--\eqref{eq:c1ffl_Z} in Section~\ref{sec:motifs}.
When $X$ steps on at $t = 0$, the direct arm immediately drives the AND-gate
factor $f_{\rm act}(X;\,K_{XZ},\,n)$ toward 1, but the second AND-gate factor
$f_{\rm act}(Y;\,K_{YZ},\,n)$ remains near zero until $Y$ rises above $K_{YZ}$.
Since $Y$ relaxes exponentially toward its steady state $Y^* = \beta_Y/\alpha_Y$
with time constant $\tau_Y = 1/\alpha_Y$, the time at which $Y$ crosses $K_{YZ}$
is approximately
\begin{equation}\label{eq:t_delay}
  t_{\rm delay} \approx \frac{1}{\alpha_Y}\ln\!\left(\frac{Y^*}{Y^* - K_{YZ}}\right),
\end{equation}
giving the sign-sensitive delay for sustained ON inputs.  For a transient
pulse of duration $\Delta t \ll 1/\alpha_Y$, $Y$ barely rises from zero before
$X$ turns off again; the AND gate never opens and $Z$ is filtered.  The delay
appears only for the ON transition (sign-sensitive), not the OFF transition, in
which $Z$ falls immediately once the direct arm is removed.  This asymmetry
allows the C1-FFL to discriminate between brief noise and sustained
environmental signals \cite{ManganAlon2003}.

\label{sec:i1ffl_analysis}

\paragraph{I1-FFL pulse analysis.}
In the incoherent type-1 FFL governed by Eqs.~\eqref{eq:i1ffl_Y}--\eqref{eq:i1ffl_Z},
let $X$ step on at $t = 0$.  Immediately after the step the repressor $Y$
is still zero, so $f_{\rm rep}(0;\,K_{YZ},\,n) = 1$ and $Z$ is driven at
its maximal rate $\beta_Z\,f_{\rm act}(X;\,K_{XZ},\,n)$.  On the time scale
$1/\alpha_Y$, $Y$ accumulates to
$Y^* = \beta_Y\,f_{\rm act}(X;\,K_{XY},\,n)/\alpha_Y$
and suppresses $Z$.  The new $Z$ steady state is
\begin{equation}
  Z^* = \frac{\beta_Z}{\alpha_Z}\,f_{\rm act}(X;\,K_{XZ},\,n)\,
         f_{\rm rep}(Y^*;\,K_{YZ},\,n) < Z_{\rm peak},
\end{equation}
so $Z$ first rises to a transient peak before falling back to $Z^*$.  The
peak height is set by the early dynamics when $Y \approx 0$, and $Z^*$ is
reduced relative to the peak by the factor $f_{\rm rep}(Y^*;\,K_{YZ},\,n)$.
The fold-change detection property \cite{Goentoro2009} follows from the
observation that if $X$ is multiplied by a constant factor $\lambda$
(fold-change), both $Y^*$ and the initial rate of $Z$ production ($dZ/dt$ at $t=0^+$) scale proportionally,
leaving the ratio $Z_{\rm peak}/Z^*$ invariant---the circuit reports the
\emph{ratio} of signal levels, not their absolute magnitudes.

\subsection{Cybernetic Allocation Objective}
\label{sec:cybernetic_derivation}
%-----------------------------------------------------------------------

The cybernetic control variables $u_i$ and $v_i$ can be derived from a
growth-rate maximization principle \cite{Kompala1984}.  At each instant the
cell must allocate a fixed total enzyme-synthesis capacity $r_E$ across $N$
pathways.  Let $u_i \geq 0$ with $\sum_i u_i = 1$ denote the synthesis
fractions, and let $v_i \in [0,1]$ denote activity fractions.  The
instantaneous growth rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:obj_cyber}
  \mu = \sum_{i=1}^{N} rG_i\,v_i,
  \qquad rG_i = \mu_{\max,i}\,\frac{E_i}{E_{\max,i}}\,\frac{S_i}{K_i + S_i}.
\end{equation}
Maximizing $\mu$ over $v_i \in [0,1]$ subject to no constraint other than
the bound gives $v_i = 1$ for the pathway with the largest $rG_i$ and
$v_i = 0$ for all others---an all-or-nothing LP solution.  The matching-law
variant smooths this discontinuous policy by solving a proportional-share
problem: the synthesis allocation $u_i$ that maximizes the expected future
return under uncertainty about which substrate will persist is
proportional to the current relative return
\begin{equation}
  u_i = \frac{rG_i}{\sum_j rG_j},
\end{equation}
which is the matching law of behavioral ecology applied to enzyme synthesis.
Enzyme activity is modulated by the normalized relative advantage,
\begin{equation}
  v_i = \frac{rG_i}{\max_j rG_j},
\end{equation}
so the best pathway operates at full activity ($v = 1$) and all others are
partially suppressed.  These two rules together constitute the cybernetic
model as implemented in \texttt{cybernetic.jl} and governed by
Eqs.~\eqref{eq:S_balance}--\eqref{eq:C_balance} of Section~\ref{sec:cybernetic}.
