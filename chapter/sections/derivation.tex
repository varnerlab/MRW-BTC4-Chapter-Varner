\section{From Mole Balances to the Flux Constraint}\label{sec:derivation}

The FBA constraint follows from a mole balance. Its derivation defines the
normalization basis and identifies the steady-state assumptions. Consider a
well-mixed culture with biomass concentration $B$ and reactor volume
$\bar V$. Their product defines the biomass basis
\begin{equation}\label{eq:biomass-basis}
  \mathcal B \equiv B\bar V,
\end{equation}
where $B$ has units gDW/L, $\bar V$ has units L, and $\mathcal B$ is the
total biomass in gDW. An intracellular concentration $C_i$ has units
mmol/gDW, and an intracellular flux $\hat v_j$ has units mmol/gDW/h.
Multiplication by $\mathcal B$ gives the total intracellular amount
$C_i\mathcal B$ and total reaction rate $\hat v_j\mathcal B$. A general
intracellular balance includes reaction, active transport, and
physical transport. Let $c_{i,s}$ be the concentration of species $i$ in a
hypothetical bulk stream $s$, and let $\dot V_s$ be its volumetric flow
rate. The intracellular mole balance is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mole-balance}
  \sum_s d_s c_{i,s}\dot V_s
  + \mathcal B\sum_j\sigma_{ij}\hat v_j
  = \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(C_i\mathcal B\bigr),
\end{equation}
where $d_s=+1$ for an inlet and $d_s=-1$ for an outlet, and
$\sigma_{ij}$ is entry $(i,j)$ of the stoichiometric matrix
$\mathbf S\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$. Each term has units mmol/h. The stream
concentration $c_{i,s}$ has units mmol/L, whereas $C_i$ has units mmol/gDW.
The cell membrane excludes bulk physical flow from the intracellular
control volume, so $\dot V_s=0$ and the first term vanishes. Active
transport remains in $\hat{\mathbf v}$ because it moves molecules without
bulk volume flow.

Expand the accumulation term before assuming a fixed reactor volume.
Substitution of $\mathcal B=B\bar V$ gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-rule}
  \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(C_i\mathcal B\bigr)
  = B\bar V\frac{dC_i}{dt}
  + C_i\bar V\frac{dB}{dt}
  + C_iB\frac{d\bar V}{dt}.
\end{equation}
After division by $\mathcal B=B\bar V$,
Equation~\eqref{eq:mole-balance} becomes
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intensive-balance}
  \frac{1}{\mathcal B}\sum_s d_s c_{i,s}\dot V_s
  + \sum_j\sigma_{ij}\hat v_j
  = \frac{dC_i}{dt}
  + \frac{C_i}{B}\frac{dB}{dt}
  + \frac{C_i}{\bar V}\frac{d\bar V}{dt}.
\end{equation}
For a batch or fed-batch culture with no biomass flow, total biomass changes
through growth. The specific growth rate is
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth-rate}
  \mu \equiv \frac{1}{\mathcal B}\frac{d\mathcal B}{dt}
  = \frac{1}{B}\frac{dB}{dt}
  + \frac{1}{\bar V}\frac{d\bar V}{dt}.
\end{equation}
The membrane removes the physical-flow term from
Equation~\eqref{eq:intensive-balance}. Balanced growth also gives
$dC_i/dt=0$. Equation~\eqref{eq:growth-rate} then reduces the balance to
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilution-constraint}
  C_i\mu = \sum_j\sigma_{ij}\hat v_j
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  \boxed{\mathbf S\hat{\mathbf v}=\mu\mathbf x},
\end{equation}
where $\mathbf x=(C_1,\dots,C_m)^\top$ contains the intracellular
concentrations in mmol/gDW and $\hat{\mathbf v}\in\mathbb R^n$ contains
the fluxes in mmol/gDW/h. Net production balances growth dilution. For a
fixed-volume batch reactor, $d\bar V/dt=0$ and
$\mu=(1/B)dB/dt$. For fed-batch operation, $d\bar V/dt\neq0$, so the
volume term must remain. The fixed-volume result is a special case of the
general balance.

Conventional FBA neglects the dilution term and uses
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sv0}
  \mathbf S\hat{\mathbf v}=\mathbf 0.
\end{equation}
This approximation is appropriate when $\mu\mathbf x$ is small relative to
the metabolic fluxes \cite{Orth2010}. Setting $\mu=0$ is not sufficient. A
cell-free batch has no growing biomass, but its transcript, protein, and
metabolite concentrations may still change. Its accumulation term
$d\mathbf x/dt$ must remain. All model classes introduced above use the
same balance but close the reaction term differently. Unstructured models
use lumped rates, structured kinetic models use state-dependent
intracellular rates, and constraint-based models use bounds and an
objective. Constraint-based reconstructions represent uptake and secretion with
transport and exchange reactions. A boundary reaction
$\emptyset\rightleftharpoons M_i$ enters $\mathbf S$ as an isolated
nonzero column with no intracellular reaction partner \cite{Bordbar2014}.
Its flux describes molecular transport across the modeled boundary, not a
reactor stream. The included boundary reactions and their bounds define the
modeled system boundary.
