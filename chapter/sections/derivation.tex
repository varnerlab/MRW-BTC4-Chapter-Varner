\section{From Mole Balances to Flux Constraints}\label{sec:derivation}

The mass-balance constraints used in FBA follow from mole balances, and their
derivation defines both the normalization basis and the steady-state
assumptions. Consider a
well-mixed culture with biomass concentration $B$ and reactor volume
$\bar V$. The biomass basis is therefore given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:biomass-basis}
  \mathcal B \equiv B\bar V,
\end{equation}
where $B$ has units gDW/L, $\bar V$ has units L, and $\mathcal B$ is the
total biomass in gDW. An intracellular concentration $C_i$ has units
mmol/gDW, and an intracellular flux $\hat v_j$ has units mmol/gDW/h.
Multiplication by the biomass basis $\mathcal B$ gives the total
intracellular amount $C_i\mathcal B$ and total reaction rate
$\hat v_j\mathcal B$. A general
intracellular balance includes reaction, active transport, and
physical transport. Let $c_{i,s}$ be the concentration of species $i$ in a
hypothetical bulk stream $s$, and let $\dot V_s$ be its volumetric flow
rate. The intracellular mole balance is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mole-balance}
  \sum_s d_s c_{i,s}\dot V_s
  + \mathcal B\sum_j\sigma_{ij}\hat v_j
  = \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(C_i\mathcal B\bigr),
\end{equation}
where the stream-direction coefficient $d_s=+1$ for an inlet and $d_s=-1$
for an outlet, and $\sigma_{ij}$ is entry $(i,j)$ of the stoichiometric matrix
$\mathbf S\in\mathbb R^{m\times n}$ for $m$ metabolites and $n$ reactions.
Each term has units mmol/h; the stream concentration $c_{i,s}$ has units
mmol/L, whereas $C_i$ has units mmol/gDW. Because the cell membrane excludes
bulk physical flow from the intracellular control volume, the volumetric flow
rate $\dot V_s=0$ and the first term vanishes, while active transport remains
in the flux vector $\hat{\mathbf v}$
because it moves molecules without bulk volume flow.

Expanding the accumulation term before assuming a fixed reactor volume and
substituting the biomass-basis relation $\mathcal B=B\bar V$ gives:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:product-rule}
  \frac{d}{dt}\bigl(C_i\mathcal B\bigr)
  = B\bar V\frac{dC_i}{dt}
  + C_i\bar V\frac{dB}{dt}
  + C_iB\frac{d\bar V}{dt}.
\end{equation}
After division by the biomass basis $\mathcal B=B\bar V$,
Equation~\eqref{eq:mole-balance} becomes:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:intensive-balance}
  \frac{1}{\mathcal B}\sum_s d_s c_{i,s}\dot V_s
  + \sum_j\sigma_{ij}\hat v_j
  = \frac{dC_i}{dt}
  + \frac{C_i}{B}\frac{dB}{dt}
  + \frac{C_i}{\bar V}\frac{d\bar V}{dt}.
\end{equation}
For a batch or fed-batch culture with no biomass flow, total biomass changes
through growth, so the specific growth rate is given by:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:growth-rate}
  \mu \equiv \frac{1}{\mathcal B}\frac{d\mathcal B}{dt}
  = \frac{1}{B}\frac{dB}{dt}
  + \frac{1}{\bar V}\frac{d\bar V}{dt}.
\end{equation}
The membrane removes the physical-flow term from
Equation~\eqref{eq:intensive-balance}. With the concentration vector defined as
$\mathbf x=(C_1,\dots,C_m)^\top$ in mmol/gDW, combining the remaining terms
with Equation~\eqref{eq:growth-rate} gives the dynamic intracellular balance:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dynamic-flux-balance}
  \mathbf S\hat{\mathbf v}
  = \frac{d\mathbf x}{dt}+\mu\mathbf x.
\end{equation}
Balanced growth gives $d\mathbf x/dt=\mathbf0$, so this balance reduces to:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:dilution-constraint}
  C_i\mu = \sum_j\sigma_{ij}\hat v_j
  \qquad\Longleftrightarrow\qquad
  \boxed{\mathbf S\hat{\mathbf v}=\mu\mathbf x},
\end{equation}
where the flux vector $\hat{\mathbf v}\in\mathbb R^n$ contains the reaction
fluxes in mmol/gDW/h, so net production balances growth dilution. For a
fixed-volume batch reactor, $d\bar V/dt=0$ and
$\mu=(1/B)dB/dt$. For fed-batch operation, $d\bar V/dt\neq0$, so the
volume term must remain. The fixed-volume result is a special case of the
general balance.

Conventional FBA neglects the dilution term and uses the following
approximation:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:sv0}
  \mathbf S\hat{\mathbf v}=\mathbf 0.
\end{equation}
This approximation is appropriate when the growth-dilution term
$\mu\mathbf x$ is small relative to the metabolic fluxes \cite{Orth2010}.
Setting the growth rate $\mu=0$ is not sufficient. A
cell-free batch has no growing biomass, but its transcript, protein, and
metabolite concentrations may still change. Its accumulation term
$d\mathbf x/dt$ must remain; on a fixed concentration basis,
Equation~\eqref{eq:dynamic-flux-balance} becomes the dynamic balance
$\mathbf S\mathbf v=d\mathbf x/dt$, where $\mathbf v$ is the cell-free flux
vector. Dynamic constraint-based models of cell-free protein synthesis have
used this form to impose time-dependent
concentration changes over short intervals \cite{Dai2018}. The same balance
underlies each model class, but each closes the reaction term differently.
Unstructured models use lumped rates, structured kinetic models use state-dependent
intracellular rates, and constraint-based models use bounds and an
objective. Constraint-based reconstructions represent uptake and secretion
with transport and exchange reactions. A boundary reaction
$\emptyset\rightleftharpoons M_i$ enters the stoichiometric matrix $\mathbf S$
as an isolated nonzero column with no intracellular reaction partner
\cite{Bordbar2014}.
Its flux describes molecular transport across the modeled boundary rather
than a reactor stream, and the included boundary reactions and their bounds
define the modeled system boundary.
