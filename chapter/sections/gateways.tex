\section{Integrative Flux Bounds}\label{sec:gateways}

Reaction bounds can include several types of condition-specific data. For
reaction $j$, write the bound as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:general-bound}
  -\delta_j\Bigl[V^{\circ}_{\max,j}\,(e/e^{\circ})\,\theta_j(\cdot)\,f_j(\cdot)\Bigr]
  \;\le\; \hat v_j \;\le\;
  V^{\circ}_{\max,j}\,(e/e^{\circ})\,\theta_j(\cdot)\,f_j(\cdot),
\end{equation}
where $V^{\circ}_{\max,j}$ is a reference capacity with units of flux. The
other factors are dimensionless. The reversibility factor $\delta_j$ sets
the reverse direction. The terms $V^{\circ}_{\max,j}$ and $f_j$ describe
enzyme capacity and substrate saturation. The ratio $(e/e^{\circ})$
describes enzyme abundance, and $\theta_j$ describes allosteric activity.
Transcriptional and translational controls $u_j$ and $w_j$ act through the
expression model and change $(e/e^{\circ})$. They are distinct from
$\theta_j$. When data are unavailable, the corresponding factor is set to
one. Measurements or mechanistic models can then replace that default.

The reversibility factor $\delta_j$ has two values:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:thermo-gate}
  \delta_j \;=\;
  \begin{cases}
    1, & \text{reaction $j$ reversible},\\[2pt]
    0, & \text{reaction $j$ irreversible},
  \end{cases}
\end{equation}
When $\delta_j=1$, the lower bound is negative and reverse flux is allowed.
When $\delta_j=0$, the lower bound is zero. A simple assignment compares
$\Delta G^{\circ}_j$ with a threshold $\Delta G^{*}_j$. This threshold rule
is heuristic. A thermodynamic analysis should instead propagate stated
activity ranges through $\Delta G=\Delta G^{\circ}+RT\ln Q$ and test
whether $\Delta G$ can change sign. eQuilibrator provides standard free
energy estimates for this calculation \cite{Beber2022}.

Enzyme kinetics set the forward capacity. Define the reference capacity as
\begin{equation}\label{eq:vmax}
  V^{\circ}_{\max,j} \;=\; k^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cat},j}\,e^{\circ},
\end{equation}
where $k^{\circ}_{\mathrm{cat},j}$ is the turnover number and $e^{\circ}$
is a reference enzyme concentration. BRENDA reports turnover numbers
\cite{Chang2021}, and BioNumbers reports cellular abundance scales
\cite{Milo2010}. The saturation factor gives the fraction of
$V^{\circ}_{\max,j}$ available at the current substrate concentration.
For a single-substrate enzyme, consider
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mm-mechanism}
  E + S_j \;\underset{k_{-1}}{\overset{k_1}{\rightleftharpoons}}\; [ES]
  \;\overset{k_{\mathrm{cat}}}{\longrightarrow}\; E + P,
\end{equation}
where $[ES]$ is the enzyme--substrate complex. The quasi-steady-state
approximation and enzyme conservation, $[E]_T=[E]+[ES]$, give
$[ES]=[E]_T[S_j]/(K_{M,j}+[S_j])$, where
$K_{M,j}=(k_{-1}+k_{\mathrm{cat}})/k_1$. With
$v_j=k_{\mathrm{cat}}[ES]$ and
$V_{\max,j}=k_{\mathrm{cat}}[E]_T$, the Michaelis--Menten law gives
\cite{MichaelisMenten1913}
\begin{equation}\label{eq:mm}
  v_j \;=\; V_{\max,j}\,\frac{[S_j]}{K_{M,j}+[S_j]}
  \qquad\Longrightarrow\qquad
  f_j([S_j]) \;=\; \frac{v_j}{V_{\max,j}}
  \;=\; \frac{[S_j]}{K_{M,j}+[S_j]}\;\in[0,1],
\end{equation}
where $[S_j]$ is substrate concentration and $K_{M,j}$ is the Michaelis
constant. The factor approaches zero at low substrate and one at
saturation. Because $f_j$ depends on $[S_j]$, the bound becomes
state-dependent.

Gene expression sets enzyme abundance. Balances for transcript $m_j$ and
protein $p_j$ are
\begin{align}
  \dot m_j &= r_{X,j}\,u_j - (\theta_{m,j}+\mu)\,m_j + \lambda_j,
  \label{eq:mrna}\\
  \dot p_j &= r_{L,j}\,w_j\,m_j - (\theta_{p,j}+\mu)\,p_j,
  \label{eq:protein}
\end{align}
where $r_{X,j}$ and $r_{L,j}$ are transcription and translation
capacities, $u_j$ and $w_j$ are dimensionless controls,
$\theta_{m,j}$ and $\theta_{p,j}$ are degradation constants, and
$\lambda_j$ is basal transcription. Growth adds $\mu$ to both loss rates.
At steady state,
\begin{equation}\label{eq:expression-ss}
  m^{\star}_j = \frac{r_{X,j}u_j+\lambda_j}{\theta_{m,j}+\mu},
  \qquad
  p^{\star}_j = \frac{r_{L,j}w_j\,m^{\star}_j}{\theta_{p,j}+\mu},
  \qquad
  (e/e^{\circ}) = \frac{p^{\star}_j}{e^{\circ}},
\end{equation}
Gene--protein--reaction rules map $p_j^\star$ to catalytic activity. These
rules represent single enzymes, multisubunit complexes, and isozymes.
Measured transcript or protein abundance can also set
$(e/e^{\circ})$ directly and produce condition-specific capacities.

A single statistical-mechanical construction can describe allosteric
activity, transcription, and translation. Divide the configurations of an
enzyme, promoter, or translation complex into active and inactive sets. The
control is the statistical weight of the active set divided by the weight of
all configurations. Let $\mathcal S_q$ be the configurations for process $q$,
where $q$ denotes
catalysis, transcription, or translation. Configuration
$s\in\mathcal S_q$ has energy $\epsilon_s$ and weight
\begin{equation}\label{eq:partition}
  \omega_s(\mathbf x)
    = g_s(\mathbf x)e^{-\beta\epsilon_s},
  \qquad
  Z_q(\mathbf x)
    = \sum_{r\in\mathcal S_q}\omega_r(\mathbf x),
  \qquad
  p_s(\mathbf x)
    = \frac{\omega_s(\mathbf x)}{Z_q(\mathbf x)},
\end{equation}
where $\beta=1/(k_BT)$ and $Z_q$ is the partition function. The factor
$g_s(\mathbf x)$ includes ligand concentration, binding stoichiometry, and
degeneracy. A state with $n$ bound effector molecules, for example, has a
concentration factor proportional to $(x/K)^n$.

Let $\mathcal A_q\subseteq\mathcal S_q$ contain the active configurations
and let $\mathcal I_q=\mathcal S_q\setminus\mathcal A_q$ contain the
inactive configurations. The control is the probability of the active set:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:active-state-control}
  \Phi_q(\mathbf x)
    = \sum_{s\in\mathcal A_q}p_s(\mathbf x)
    = \frac{\displaystyle\sum_{s\in\mathcal A_q}
        g_s(\mathbf x)e^{-\beta\epsilon_s}}
      {\displaystyle\sum_{r\in\mathcal S_q}
        g_r(\mathbf x)e^{-\beta\epsilon_r}}
    = \frac{Z_{\mathcal A_q}}
      {Z_{\mathcal A_q}+Z_{\mathcal I_q}}.
\end{equation}
Here $Z_{\mathcal B}=\sum_{s\in\mathcal B}\omega_s$ is the partition sum
over subset $\mathcal B$. Active states appear in the numerator and
denominator; inactive states appear only in the denominator. Therefore
$0\leq\Phi_q\leq1$. An effector increases or decreases $\Phi_q$ according
to the states it stabilizes. Choose configuration $0$ as a reference and define
$\Delta\epsilon_s=\epsilon_s-\epsilon_0$. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:effective-state-weight}
  K_{\star,s}
    \equiv \frac{\omega_s/g_s}{\omega_0/g_0}
    = e^{-\beta\Delta\epsilon_s},
  \qquad
  \frac{\omega_s}{\omega_0}
    = \frac{g_s(\mathbf x)}{g_0(\mathbf x)}K_{\star,s}.
\end{equation}
With $\omega_0=g_0=1$, the state weight is
$\omega_s=g_s(\mathbf x)K_{\star,s}$. A term such as $K_2f_{TL}$ combines
the energetic factor $K_2=e^{-\beta\Delta\epsilon_2}$ with the available
ligand-bound transcription factor $f_{TL}$. Fitted values of $K_1$ and
$K_2$ may also include constant polymerase concentration, standard-state
terms, or other fixed contributions. They need not be measured
dissociation constants.

Moon, Voigt, and coworkers used this construction for inducible promoters
\cite{Moon2012}. The Lux promoter has weights $1$, $K_1$, and
$K_2f_{TL}$ for the empty, polymerase-bound, and polymerase--LuxR-bound
states. Both polymerase-bound states permit transcription. The Tac promoter
has weights $1$, $K_1$, and $K_2f_T$ for the empty, polymerase-bound, and
repressor-bound states. Only the polymerase-bound state permits
transcription. Normalization gives
\begin{equation}\label{eq:moon-promoter-control}
  u_{\mathrm{Lux}}
    = \frac{K_1+K_2f_{TL}}{1+K_1+K_2f_{TL}},
  \qquad
  u_{\mathrm{Tac}}
    = \frac{K_1}{1+K_1+K_2f_T}.
\end{equation}
Here $f_{TL}$ is the ligand-bound transcription-factor fraction and $f_T$
is the ligand-free fraction. Increasing inducer adds weight to an active
Lux state and removes weight from an inactive Tac state. Both controls are
special cases of Equation~\eqref{eq:active-state-control}. A two-state model
gives a familiar special case. If the inactive state has
weight $\omega_I=1$ and the effector-stabilized active state has weight
$\omega_A=(x/K)^n e^{-\beta\Delta\epsilon}$, then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:two-state-control}
  \Phi_q(x)
    = \frac{(x/K)^n e^{-\beta\Delta\epsilon}}
      {1+(x/K)^n e^{-\beta\Delta\epsilon}},
\end{equation}
where $\Delta\epsilon$ is the active-state energy relative to the inactive
state. If the effector instead stabilizes the inactive state, its weight
appears only in the denominator and the control decreases with $x$.

Equation~\eqref{eq:active-state-control} gives each process-specific factor:
\begin{equation}\label{eq:control-mapping}
  \theta_j = \Phi_{\mathrm{cat},j},
  \qquad
  \bar u_j = \Phi_{\mathrm{TX},j},
  \qquad
  w_j = \Phi_{\mathrm{TL},j}.
\end{equation}
The factor $\theta_j$ multiplies catalytic capacity in
Equation~\eqref{eq:general-bound}. The factor $\bar u_j$ controls
transcription, and $w_j$ controls translation in
Equation~\eqref{eq:protein}. These controls are distinct from the
degradation constants $\theta_{m,j}$ and $\theta_{p,j}$. Split the
transcriptionally active set into regulated and basal states.
Let $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{TX}}^{\mathrm{reg}}$ contain the regulated states
and $\mathcal A_{\mathrm{TX}}^{\mathrm{basal}}$ contain states that support
transcription without the regulated input. Then
\begin{equation}\label{eq:control-split}
  \bar u_j
    = \frac{Z_{\mathcal A_{\mathrm{TX}}^{\mathrm{reg}}}}{Z_{\mathrm{TX}}}
    + \frac{Z_{\mathcal A_{\mathrm{TX}}^{\mathrm{basal}}}}{Z_{\mathrm{TX}}}
    = u_j + u_j^{\dagger},
  \qquad
  \lambda_j \equiv r_{X,j}u_j^{\dagger}.
\end{equation}
The regulated term $u_j$ enters Equation~\eqref{eq:mrna}. The basal term
$u_j^{\dagger}$ defines $\lambda_j$. Moon, Voigt, and coworkers used this
promoter-state construction \cite{Moon2012}. Adhikari and coworkers
extended it to transcriptional and translational control
\cite{Adhikari2020}.

These factors require thermodynamic, kinetic, expression, and regulatory
data. When data are unavailable, set the correction factors to one,
$f_j\approx1$, $(e/e^{\circ})\approx1$, and
$\theta_j\approx1$. This gives the baseline bound
\begin{equation}\label{eq:simplified-bound}
  -\delta_j\,V^{\circ}_{\max,j} \;\le\; \hat v_j \;\le\; V^{\circ}_{\max,j}.
\end{equation}
Equation~\eqref{eq:simplified-bound} retains only reversibility and
reference capacity. Equation~\eqref{eq:general-bound} shows which omitted
factors require additional data. Each factor has a precedent in
constraint-based modeling. Thermodynamics-
based flux analysis uses free energies and metabolite activity ranges to
exclude infeasible states \cite{Henry2007}. Enzyme-constrained FBA uses
turnover numbers and enzyme abundance; GECKO applies this approach at genome
scale \cite{Sanchez2017}. Metabolism-and-expression models couple
transcription and translation to metabolism \cite{OBrien2013}. Regulatory
FBA uses Boolean expression rules \cite{Covert2001}, whereas PROM uses
data-derived interaction probabilities \cite{Chandrasekaran2010}. The
allosteric factor $\theta_j$ represents direct regulation of enzyme
activity. Equation~\eqref{eq:general-bound} places these established terms
in one notation. The balances above use aggregate transcription and
translation capacities,
$r_{X,j}$ and $r_{L,j}$. A sequence-resolved model can instead calculate
nucleotide, amino-acid, and energy demands from each gene and protein
sequence and add those reactions to $\mathbf S$ \cite{AllenPalsson2003}.
Expression then becomes part of the flux calculation. Vilkhovoy and
coworkers used this construction for cell-free protein synthesis
\cite{Vilkhovoy2018}; the later capstone returns to that model.
